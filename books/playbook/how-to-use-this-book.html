How to Use This Book

The Product Manager's Playbook · 2 min read

The book is organized in five parts that follow the life of a product, from the first hint of a problem to a business that scales.

  • Part 1, Foundations (Chapters 1 to 3): what the job is now, how to find problems worth solving, and how to validate them before you spend real money.
  • Part 2, Strategy (Chapters 4 to 7): vision and positioning, prioritization and roadmaps, the financial side of product decisions, and managing more than one product.
  • Part 3, Building (Chapters 8 to 11): the product team, working with engineering and design, using data well, and building trust through security, privacy and compliance.
  • Part 4, Customers (Chapters 12 to 14): designing with empathy, taking a product to market, and keeping and growing customers.
  • Part 5, Growth (Chapters 15 to 18): partnerships, scaling, building AI products, and an integrated playbook that ties it all together.

You can read it front to back, or jump to the chapter that matches the problem on your desk. Each chapter stands on its own, and cross-references point you to related material.

Meet Brindlepath

Most chapters begin with a scene from Brindlepath, a fictional company that sells care-coordination software to health systems and large physician groups. Its product helps nurse navigators and care managers track patients as they move between the hospital, specialists, home health and primary care, the handoffs where things most often fall through the cracks.

Brindlepath is invented. So are its people: Maya Smith, who joins as Vice President of Product in Chapter 1; Dev Raman, the engineering lead; Lena Park, the head of design; and the customers, clinicians and executives they work with. Brindlepath, its people, its customers and every event in its story are fictional. They are not based on any real company, client, employer or person, and any resemblance to real organizations or people is coincidental. The situations are composites of challenges that are common across the software industry, written to illustrate the ideas in each chapter. Following one company through the whole book shows how the decisions connect: the problem you choose in Chapter 2 shapes the pricing you can charge in Chapter 6, which shapes the team you can afford in Chapter 8.

Features in every chapter

  • How AI changes this. What AI tools and agents can take on in that part of the work, what they cannot, and the traps to avoid.
  • Figures. Frameworks and charts you can reuse. Charts built on real-world data cite their source. Charts that use made-up numbers to teach a point are labelled illustrative.
  • Chapter checklist. A one-page summary of the questions to ask and the work to do, for when you come back to the chapter months later with a real decision to make.

Templates for the most useful artifacts, including a problem statement, a product requirements document, a launch checklist and a one-page roadmap, are collected in the Appendix.
