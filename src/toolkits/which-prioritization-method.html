Which prioritization method?

Pick the lightest method that gives you a decision you can defend. Two questions decide it.

1Are the options the same kind of thing, in one product area?
No

Weighted scoring

Different kinds of bets, or a strategy that's shifting. Agree the criteria and weights first, then score.
2If yes: can you estimate reach with some confidence?
Yes

RICE

Similar items for the same users, where you know roughly how many each one reaches.
No, it's a guess

ICE

Lighter, and more honest than RICE built on guessed reach.

The three methods at a glance

RICE

Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort
Scales
Impact 3 / 2 / 1 / 0.5 / 0.25 · Confidence 100 / 80 / 50% · Effort in person-months
Best for
Ordering many comparable items
Watch out
Strategy, revenue and risk aren't in the formula; guessed reach looks like data

Weighted scoring

Σ weight × score (1–5), costs inverted
Scales
Your own criteria, weights 0–5; effort and risk count lower-is-better
Best for
Different bets; making strategy explicit
Watch out
Whoever picks the criteria can tilt the result

ICE

Impact × Confidence × Ease
Scales
Each 1–10 (or 1–5), scored quickly by the team
Best for
Fast triage when reach is unknown
Watch out
Subjective: two people can score the same idea very differently
Use them together. Each quarter, weighted scoring picks the themes and bets, with leadership agreeing the weights. Within each theme, RICE (or ICE) orders the work week to week. If the two disagree, that's the conversation to have.

Six rules for a ranking you can defend

  1. Agree the weights before anyone scores. The weights are the strategy.
  2. Treat close calls as ties. 66.7 vs 64.2 isn't a real difference; decide on evidence.
  3. Keep any one criterion under half the weight, or you're really using one criterion.
  4. Low confidence means a cheap test, not a commitment.
  5. The score starts the decision; it doesn't make it. Timing, context and judgement still decide.
  6. Re-run it each quarter. Goals change, and the weights should too.
