# Part 4: Customers

# 12. Designing with Empathy

**The big idea.** Products succeed or fail in the user's real environment, not in the lab. Empathy is a method: understand context, goals and constraints by watching real work.

**Key points**

- Physicians spend **nearly two hours on the health record and desk work for every hour with patients** (Sinsky, 2016). Every product either adds to that burden or relieves it.
- **Contextual inquiry and shadowing** find what lab testing misses.
- **Journey maps** make pain points visible to the whole team. **Personas** must summarize evidence.
- Design for **interruption,** prevent errors and **offer undo,** keep the screen still, minimize clicks.
- Remember **users who never log in,** such as patients, students and customers.
- Trust users' **problems;** treat their proposed solutions as one input.
- Design AI features around **the reviewer's time.**

![Where a physician's office day goes. Source: Sinsky et al., 2016.](../fig/12-1.png){width=100%}

**Checklist**

- [ ] Has your trio watched users in their real environment this quarter?
- [ ] Do you have a journey map built from field research?
- [ ] Does the product survive interruptions and make errors easy to undo?
- [ ] Do design reviews consider people who never log in?
- [ ] Do you track usability measures for key tasks?

**Try this week.** Arrange half a day watching a user at work for each member of the trio. Bring back one workaround, one frustration and one moment the product helped.

# 13. Go-to-Market and Launch

**The big idea.** Building the product is half the job. A launch gets value into customers' hands and heads, with the right message for each audience and teams ready to carry it.

**Key points**

- A GTM plan answers **who, what message, which channels, what price, how customers succeed and how you'll measure it.**
- Choose a motion, or blend: **sales-led, product-led or partner-led.**
- Write **messaging per role:** the problem they feel, the outcome they want, the proof they need.
- **Tier launches** and decide the tier when the work is planned.
- **Enable sales and success** with the story, a demo, objections, boundaries and practice.
- **Price pilots,** design them for expansion, and agree the expansion terms in advance.
- In regulated markets plan for **approvals, training, evidence for claims and procurement timelines.**

![Launch tiers: match the effort to the stakes.](../fig/13-2.png){width=100%}

**Checklist**

- [ ] Is there messaging for each role in the buying group?
- [ ] Is each launch tiered early?
- [ ] Are sellers enabled with practice, not just slides?
- [ ] Do pilots have a fee and expansion terms?
- [ ] Do you measure adoption at 30, 60 and 90 days?

**Try this week.** Ask a salesperson to demo your next release to you cold. Every question they can't answer goes into your enablement plan.

# 14. Customer Success and Retention

**The big idea.** Subscription businesses win at renewal, every month. Customer success works proactively so customers achieve the outcomes they bought, and retention compounds.

**Key points**

- **Support** fixes problems; **customer success** drives outcomes.
- Retention compounds: $10 million at 90 percent net retention becomes about **$5.9 million** in five years; at 120 percent, about **$24.9 million.**
- The life cycle: **onboard, adopt, realize value, renew, expand.**
- **Time to first value** has the biggest effect on whether customers stay.
- **Health scores** should weight outcomes and adoption most. Friendliness can hide a failing deployment.
- Run **business reviews** around the customer's outcomes, with the executive sponsor in the room.
- Feed customer evidence into product, and **close the loop** with customers whose ideas you used.

![Retention compounds (illustrative).](../fig/14-1.png){width=100%}

**Checklist**

- [ ] Do you track net and gross revenue retention?
- [ ] Is time to first value defined and tracked?
- [ ] Does every at-risk account have an owner and a plan?
- [ ] Do success and product teams meet regularly?
- [ ] Do you build advocacy with permission and accurate claims?

**Try this week.** For your five largest customers, check the outcome trend, active users by site and the executive sponsor. Anyone slipping gets an owner and a plan.
