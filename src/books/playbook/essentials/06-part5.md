# Part 5: Growth

# 15. Partnerships and Ecosystems

**The big idea.** Good partnerships create value for both sides every day, in the normal course of their work. Partnerships that depend on goodwill fade.

**Key points**

- Partner to **complete the product, extend reach, strengthen the outcome or accelerate innovation.**
- Types: **technology and integration, platform and marketplace, channel, services, and strategic co-development.**
- Assess **mutual value, customer overlap, workflow fit, incentive alignment, commitment and risk.**
- Start with a **pilot,** name **owners on both sides,** agree shared measures.
- Ecosystems grow from **open, documented interfaces,** a partner program and shared success stories.
- **Monitor integrations,** diversify away from single platforms, and end partnerships well.
- Treat **AI model providers** as strategic partners, and keep the ability to switch.

![Five kinds of partnership.](../fig/15-1.png){width=100%}

**Checklist**

- [ ] Can you state what both sides gain from each partnership?
- [ ] Do partners' incentives reward working with you?
- [ ] Are data agreements in place before data flows?
- [ ] Are partner integrations monitored?
- [ ] Could you switch AI model providers?

**Try this week.** Score each partner on overlap, fit, incentives and commitment. For any you can't write the partner's gain for, have a frank conversation.

# 16. Scaling the Product and the Company

**The big idea.** Scaling means changing how the product and company work, so growth makes things better instead of breaking them. The most important product decision is how you handle customer differences.

**Key points**

- **Configure, don't customize.** Push customer differences toward standard, configurable and extensible options.
- Scale technology with **multi-tenancy, standard integrations, service level objectives, error budgets and observability.**
- Protect quality with **capacity allocation,** automation, flags and blameless reviews.
- Make compliance systematic: **certifications early,** compliance built into the platform.
- Expand **one direction at a time,** from a strong core.
- **Conway's law:** teams around customer journeys produce products without seams.
- AI changes the economics of scale, but **AI costs grow with use.**

![From standard to custom.](../fig/16-1.png){width=100%}

**Checklist**

- [ ] Do you have a rule for customer-specific requests?
- [ ] Do you have SLOs and an error budget?
- [ ] Will monitoring catch silent integration failures?
- [ ] Are teams organized around customer journeys?
- [ ] Is cost per customer, including AI, tracked?

**Try this week.** List every customer-specific setting and code path. Mark each: make it configuration for all, move it to a partner, or retire it.

# 17. Building AI Products and Leading with AI Agents

**The big idea.** Impressive AI demos are easy; reliable AI products are not. Start with the problem, match autonomy to the cost of an error, evaluate rigorously, and lead agents like accountable team members.

**Key points**

- **88 percent** of organizations use AI somewhere, but only **23 percent** are scaling AI agents (McKinsey, 2025). The gap is where product leadership matters.
- AI fits language, variety and drafts where errors can be caught. It fits poorly where answers must be exact and unreviewed.
- Climb the **autonomy ladder** (assist, suggest, act with approval, act within limits, act autonomously) only on evidence.
- Design for trust: **sources, uncertainty, easy correction, control and clear labels.**
- **Evaluation** separates demos from products: a realistic set, expert criteria, thresholds, gated releases and production monitoring.
- **Ground** AI in the right data, watch **cost and speed,** and guard against bias, manipulation and privacy risk.
- Give every agent a **job description, an owner, limited permissions and regular review.** Humans stay accountable for decisions.

![The autonomy ladder.](../fig/17-2.png){width=100%}

**Checklist**

- [ ] Is each AI feature solving a validated problem?
- [ ] Is autonomy matched to the consequences of being wrong?
- [ ] Does an evaluation set with thresholds exist before the feature?
- [ ] Is quality monitored in production?
- [ ] Does every agent have an owner and limited permissions?

**Try this week.** Write the evaluation plan for one AI feature. If you can't define the evaluation set and criteria, build those before another line of the feature.

# 18. The Integrated Playbook

**The big idea.** The pieces only work together. Problem, strategy, numbers, team, trust, customers, partners and AI form one system, held together by a steady operating rhythm.

**Key points**

- Decisions in one area shape every other: the problem you choose sets the price you can charge, which sets the team you can afford.
- The **four pillars** run through every part of the work.
- Keep a rhythm: **weekly** learning, **monthly** results, **quarterly** investment, **yearly** strategy.
- In a new role, spend **30 days listening, 30 diagnosing and aligning, 30 deciding and communicating.**
- The core of the job, understanding people, choosing well, building trust and owning the outcome, **stays human.**

![The integrated playbook.](../fig/18-1.png){width=100%}

**Checklist**

- [ ] Do you consider how each decision affects the whole system?
- [ ] Do you keep a weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly rhythm?
- [ ] Is your strategy on one page, refreshed yearly?
- [ ] Do you look at the results, and change course when they tell you to?

**Try this week.** Put the operating rhythm on your calendar for the next quarter, starting with one customer conversation for every trio this week.
