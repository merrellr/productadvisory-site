What this is

Practical tools built from real product work

These are the same tools we use with clients. Each one works right in your browser, and the decision templates also come as a fillable PDF with a matching Google Sheet or Excel file, so you can work on screen, in a spreadsheet, or on your phone. Every tool includes a worked example and clear instructions, so you are never starting from a blank page.

Why it matters

Better decisions, easier to defend

Most product decisions are made fast, with opinions in the room and little written down. That works until someone asks why. A simple structure gets the real criteria on the table, shows where your team disagrees, and leaves a record you can stand behind later. These tools give you that structure in about an hour, not a week.

How to use it

Three steps to a decision you can defend

  1. Pick a tool that fits your level or the problem in front of you.
  2. Look at the example first. Spend five minutes with it before you fill in your own.
  3. Fill it in with your team. Have two people score on their own, then compare. The gaps are where the real conversation is.

Feature Prioritization Scorecard Interactive

StarterPractitionerLeader
  • Time needed: About 20 minutes
  • Formats: Interactive in your browser, or an Excel template (works in Google Sheets)

Rank features with criteria you can change, or with RICE, and compare the two rankings side by side, so your roadmap reflects your goals and not the loudest voice. Weight what matters, score each feature, and watch the ranking update as you go.

Use it when: the backlog is longer than the quarter, and every stakeholder thinks their feature is first.

You walk away with: a ranked list, the criteria behind it, and a link or file you can share.

Build, Buy, or Partner Decision Matrix New · Interactive

PractitionerLeaderExecutive
  • Time needed: About 30 to 60 minutes
  • Formats: Interactive in your browser, fillable PDF, Google Sheet or Excel

Decide whether to build a capability yourself, buy it, or partner for it. Score the options against ten criteria, compare the real three-year cost, and record your decision with its risks and tripwires.

Use it when: You are weighing a platform, a vendor, a new feature area, or an AI capability and the team is split.

You walk away with: A scored comparison, a cost view, and a written decision you can share.
